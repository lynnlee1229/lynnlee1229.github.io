About

Lynn Lee, a data engineer working on advertising data, stream processing and practical AI agents.

Hi, I’m Lynn Lee.

I’m a Data Engineer at Tencent Ads, working on advertising user-behavior data, real-time processing and data quality. My background is in spatiotemporal computing; today, I focus on making complex data pipelines observable, verifiable and recoverable.

This blog is my engineering notebook. I write about the assumptions behind a design, the trade-offs it makes, the ways it can fail, and the evidence needed to trust it.

What I work on

Advertising data and stream processing

I contribute to upgrades of advertising real-time data platforms, including the migration of legacy Java and Flink jobs into standardized pipelines. My work spans migration reconciliation, event enrichment, deduplication, operational monitoring and compute-resource optimization.

International data infrastructure

I work on AWS-based advertising user-behavior pipelines for international markets: integrating heterogeneous media sources, supporting streaming and batch processing, and mapping source protocols into shared data contracts. I have worked with S3, Iceberg and MSK, with particular attention to configuration-driven ingestion and safe schema changes.

AI agent engineering

I explore agents for operations and data investigation, connecting alert analysis, evidence retrieval, diagnosis and queries through traceable workflows. My experience includes LangChain and AWS Bedrock, alongside specification-driven development, test-driven development and reusable skills for recurring engineering tasks.

During university, I received a first prize in the China Undergraduate Mathematical Contest in Modeling and a Finalist distinction in MCM/ICM. That experience shaped a habit I still rely on: make the assumptions and evaluation criteria explicit before choosing an implementation.

How I approach engineering

  • Define correctness first. Agree on inputs, outputs, metric definitions and failure semantics.
  • Make decisions with evidence. Use reconciliation, replay, load tests and execution plans to test assumptions.
  • Keep changes recoverable. Roll out in small steps with traceability and a rollback path.
  • Turn repetition into tools. Use scripts, workflows and agents for repeatable work that can be checked.
